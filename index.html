Doctoral Thesis

Adaptive Human-Agent Dialogues for Collaborative Reasoning and Decision-making

    Enabling human-agent collaboration, to reason and make decisions collaboratively is challenging. The system must be able to participate in a purposeful dialogue that includes ability to reference to items mentioned earlier in the current dialogue and also to keep track of different sub-dialogues. It must also know the topic and the main goal of a dialogue to be able to make appropriate moves. The major challenge is to endow these capabilities that allow them to take a role of a pal (friend) that is usually only taken by a human, thus, making an agent less as a tool that people use and more as a peer or pal that people can collaborate with. Thus, the whole interaction process is a dynamic one that relies on the capabilities of the agent to personalise and adapt to the human.

    The following are the research questions directing my research:

    1. What knowledge is required to enable dialogue-based human-agent collaboration?
    2. How can a software agent reason and make decisions, in collaboration with a human?
    3. How can a software agent evaluate its own behavior?
    4. How are the collaborative dialogues perceived by humans?

    The following are the objectives:

    • Objective 1: Identify necessary knowledge and develop a knowledge base that can function as a belief base for a cognitive companion / coach
    • Objective 2: Develop heuristics for adaptation, reasoning and decision making
    • Objective 3: Develop methods for agent's self evaluation
    • Objective 4: Conduct empirical user studies to evaluate the results

    The main contributions are:

    • An instantiated BDI-architecture for a cognitive companion where the developed knowledge base includes knowledge about the human, domain, itself and conducting dialogue activity (Beliefs), the developed heuristics for reasoning and decision making are used for planning (Intentions), goal setting and adaptation (Desires).
    • A method for combining four types of argument-based dialogues with different purposes within the same framework of collaborative dialogue activity.
    • Results regarding how humans perceive the behaviour of the cognitive companion / coach.
